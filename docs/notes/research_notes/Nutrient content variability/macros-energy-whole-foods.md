# Variability of energy and macronutrients per 100 g in whole/unprocessed foods

Note: cost-limited run (8 tool calls). Several searches returned only program descriptions; the USDA SR Legacy / FoodData Central n, SE, min and max values were NOT retrieved directly. Gaps are flagged.

## What CVs / SDs / ranges have been reported within a single food?

### Takeaway
The only hard within-food numbers found are for retail Cavendish bananas (min-max by ripeness, n=5-7) and a few milk and salmon figures. Per-nutrient CVs from USDA NFNAP/SR were not retrieved. The only generic CV figures found are old assumed values (energy 10-30%, protein 10-20%, other nutrients 10-45%).

### Cited Findings
- Banana, retail Cavendish, g/100 g, mean (min-max), n (no SDs given in the table):
  - Starch: slightly ripe 4.50 (3.39-5.18) n=7; ripe 2.52 (1.55-5.40) n=7; overripe 0.48 (0.30-0.86) n=6.
  - Sucrose: slightly ripe 3.33 (1.84-5.25); ripe 4.56 (1.15-9.40); overripe 1.88 (0.53-4.31).
  - Fructose: 6.08 (5.26-6.72); 6.23 (3.33-9.43); 6.72 (5.73-7.38).
  - Glucose: 6.03 (5.18-6.78); 5.33 (<0.2-9.49); 6.96 (6.12-7.84).
  - Total sugars (glucose+fructose+sucrose): 15.0 (12.9-16.6); 17.1 (14.4-19.4); 16.7 (14.2-19.6).
  - Fibre (AOAC 2011.25 mEG): 4.99 (3.84-6.32) n=5; 3.69 (2.66-5.12); 1.93 (1.55-2.44).
  - Moisture: 75.57 (74.97-77.02); 75.27 (72.15-76.92); 78.58 (75.87-80.56).
  — [Banana ripeness study, PMC8266066, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8266066/) (retrieved via fetch summary; table values not independently re-checked)
- Banana, single-lot ripening study (3 samples per stage, each a homogenate of 3-5 fruit): fibre ~18 g/100 g unripe, falling ~14 g/100 g by slightly ripe; starch down 9.3 g/100 g unripe to slightly ripe; moisture 74.8 (unripe) to 80.3 (overripe); sucrose <1 unripe, ~2.5 slightly ripe/ripe; glucose and fructose ~5.0 g/100 g higher than unripe — [PMC8266066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8266066/)
- Banana, other studies (different bases, not comparable): starch 21.8-24.7% at stage 1 falling to 0.3-0.6% at stage 11 in five cultivars (source is a patent document; its sugar figures are internally inconsistent, treat with caution) — [USPTO PP20645](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/PP20645); flour total starch 76.2% (stage I) to 25.3% (stage VII), resistant starch 34.6% to 8.8% (probably dry basis) — [Carbohydr. Polym. Technol. Appl. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/5cc6def554ba4898bed54b8252ea3f66)
- Generic CV assumptions (not measured NFNAP data): energy ~10-30%, protein ~10-20%, other nutrients ~10-45%, attributed to a 1985 personal communication. The same source notes USDA gives no CV for energy because energy values are computed, and that CV = 100 x SD / mean with SD = SE x sqrt(n) can be derived from SR's n and SE — [NAP appendix](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/618/chapter/17)
- USDA beef NDI study: 98 Choice and Select cuts, raw and cooked, 8-48 analytical samples per item, means and SDs calculated from individual samples (the SDs themselves were not retrieved) — [USDA ARS documentation](https://ars.usda.gov/ARSUSERFILES/80400535/DATA/BEEF/USDA%20SELECT%20AND%20CHOICE%20BEEF/USDA%20SELECT%20AND%20CHOICE%20BEEF%20CUTS%20DOCUMENTATION.DOCX)
- USDA ground beef: labelled 3-30% fat samples were used to build regression-based nutrient values across that range (fat is effectively a product specification) — [search result summary, USDA NDL study, via ARS beef pages](https://ars.usda.gov/northeast-area/beltsville-md-bhnrc/beltsville-human-nutrition-research-center/methods-and-application-of-food-composition-laboratory/mafcl-site-pages/beef-study-usda-select-and-usda-choice-cuts)
- NFNAP scale: more than 2,600 sample units of 74 foods from 12 locations in FY2013 — [USDA CRIS project page](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/0423578-national-food-and-nutrient-analysis-program.html)

## How does within-food variability compare with between-cultivar/breed/origin/season variability?

### Takeaway
Only milk and salmon give usable numbers. Breed differences in milk (fat ~1.3 percentage points between Jersey and Holstein) and the growth-stage/season differences in salmon fat (several-fold) are far larger than the typical sample-to-sample scatter of basic macronutrients.

### Cited Findings
- Milk, Vermont herd study: Jersey 5.20% fat and 3.83% protein vs Holstein 3.91% fat and 3.12% protein (P<0.001) — [UVM ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uvm.edu/items/867970df-8fed-467e-ba2f-39f1980b9461)
- Milk, Brazilian commercial herds: no significant breed effect on fat, but Jersey 3.97% vs Holstein 3.54%; month of sampling significantly affected crude and true protein — [RVZ article](https://rvz.emnuvens.com.br/rvz/article/download/1144/685/4438)
- Milk, Swedish bulk milk: Jersey fat 2.2-2.5 percentage points higher than other breeds; season had limited effect — [SLU thesis](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/15823/)
- Milk, Turkish bulk milk: fat and protein highest in autumn/winter, lowest in spring/summer, but no significant seasonal change on large-scale farms — [Agrojournal](https://www.agrojournal.org/23/06-24.html)
- Milk, Irish study of 60 cows (Jersey, Elite Holstein-Friesian, national-average HF): mean 4.72 +/- 1.30% fat, 3.85 +/- 0.61% protein (implied CV about 28% for fat and 16% for protein among individual cows, my calculation) — [Teagasc](https://t-stor.teagasc.ie/entities/publication/57fa2a2f-8cb4-44a1-9df5-2728f39d8442)
- Atlantic salmon fillet fat rose from 3-4% to 17-19% across a year-long trial, mostly July-November (12-13 percentage points), in both 0+ and 1+ salmon (this is growth/feeding stage as well as season) — [Nofima](https://nofima.com/publication/747198/)
- Fat also varies within one salmon fillet (tail lowest in EPA/DHA) — [Food Chemistry 2026 per search summary](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/nutritional-parameters-of-dorsal-and-ventral-fillets-in-summer-an/)
- Beef cut effect: in USDA Prime cuts, raw separable lean lipid was significantly higher in rib roast and strip loin than in tenderloin and top sirloin (no numbers retrieved) — [PMC11397233](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11397233/)

### Inferences
- For banana, ripeness stage moves starch from ~4.5 to ~0.5 g/100 g (about 9-fold) but total sugars stay ~13-19 g/100 g across the retail stages, so total carbohydrate and energy per 100 g change much less than the starch/sugar split. Moisture varies only ~72-81 g/100 g.
- The wide ripe-stage range (starch 1.55-5.40, sucrose 1.15-9.40) indicates that "ripe" is a coarse label and sample-to-sample scatter within a stage is large for the starch/sugar split.
- Fat (meat, fish, milk) is the most variable macronutrient; carbohydrate and protein in fruit and meat are much more stable, and energy variability mostly follows fat and water.

### Gaps
- No SR Legacy/FoodData Central n, SE, min, max values (potato, rice, apple, egg, pork, beef) were retrieved; CVs would need to be computed from the SR files directly (SD = SE x sqrt(n)).
- No NFNAP-specific CVs, no potato/rice/apple cultivar data, no egg or herring/mackerel figures, and no beef/pork fat SDs were found within the call budget.

## What are the causes, and which dominate for which food groups?

### Takeaway
Supported causes from the sources found: ripeness (banana starch, sugars, fibre, moisture), breed (milk fat, protein), season and feeding/growth stage (salmon and milk fat), cut (beef lipid), and product specification (ground beef fat). Other causes (cultivar, storage, trimming, analytical method) were not quantified here.

### Cited Findings
- Banana: ripening converts starch to sugars and sucrose then falls in overripe fruit; fibre (mEG) falls from ~18 to 4-5 g/100 g, with moisture rising in overripe fruit — [PMC8266066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8266066/)
- Starch and sugar transformation differs between banana and plantain cultivars grown in Cameroon (title only; values not retrieved) — [CIRAD record](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/dig-cirad-fr-391888)
- Milk: breed is the most consistent driver; season effect is smaller and herd/feeding dependent (see studies above).
- Salmon: fat driven by feeding and growth stage, and fillet position (see above).
- Beef: cut (rib and strip loin higher lipid than tenderloin and sirloin) — [PMC11397233](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11397233/)

### Inferences
- Dominant sources by group (inferred): fruit, ripeness and moisture; meat, cut/trim and animal fatness; fish, fat cycle; milk, breed then feed/season. Not verified for vegetables, grains, eggs.

### Gaps
- Nothing found on grains, potatoes, eggs, apples, or analytical-method contribution to variance.

## Rules of thumb for the uncertainty of a food composition table value

### Takeaway
No explicit rule-of-thumb percentage from Greenfield & Southgate or FAO/INFOODS was found. Related benchmarks exist (label tolerances, table-to-table differences, EuroFIR quality framework).

### Cited Findings
- EuroFIR uses an ISO 9001-based quality framework grading data on food description, component identification, sampling, sample handling, analytical method and analytical performance — [Roe and Finglas, Food Nutr Res](https://foodandnutritionresearch.net/index.php/fnr/article/download/500/744)
- Greenfield & Southgate is the FAO standard reference on generating, compiling and using composition data (summary page only; manual not read) — [EuroFIR Synthesis Report 7](https://www.eurofir.org/wp-admin/wp-content/uploads/EuroFIR%20synthesis%20reports/EuroFIR_Synthesis_Report_No_7_Food_Composition_Explained.pdf)
- UK label tolerance for macronutrients: more than 5% content, +/-20% of declared value; 2-5%, +/-30% (as stated by the Measurement Toolkit; verify against regulation) — [Measurement Toolkit](https://measurement-toolkit.org/diet/introduction/food-composition)
- British vs American (Chilean) tables: mean macronutrient intake estimates differed by 5.3-8.9% — [Public Health Nutrition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5D246886C6B5CC6A44ACF3A6C6F3A2EE)

### Inferences
- A conservative working range for a single table value of a fatty/variable food is plausibly tens of percent, but this is not supported by a retrieved source and should not be quoted as sourced.

### Gaps
- Greenfield & Southgate text and FAO/INFOODS guidance on expected uncertainty were not accessed.

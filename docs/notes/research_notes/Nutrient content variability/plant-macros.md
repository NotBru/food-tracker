# Variability of energy and macronutrients per unit weight in plant foods, and its causes

Scope note: budget-limited run (about 15 tool calls). Primary papers were fetched where possible (Moreno 2021 banana, Mosleth 2020 wheat, Greveniotis 2023 faba bean, Lee 2022 rice). Other items are from search-result summaries of primary papers and are marked "(search summary)". Many crops in the brief (grapes, citrus, maize, oats, nuts, most vegetables) were **not** reached; see Gaps. The earlier lite note (`../Nutrient content variability/macros-energy-whole-foods.md`) already holds the retail Cavendish ripeness table (PMC8266066); it is only referred to here, not repeated.

## Banana: carbohydrate, starch/sugar split, water and dry matter by cultivar, origin, ripeness and storage

### Takeaway
The largest differences are between cultivar groups. Plantain and cooking-banana pulp has much more dry matter and starch than dessert (AAA) bananas, and plantains keep undegraded starch when ripe. Within one dessert cultivar, ripening mostly turns starch into sugar and changes total carbohydrate per 100 g only a little. Moisture changes by only a few g/100 g (about 72-81 g/100 g at retail stages, from the earlier note). No usable per-origin (country) data for Cavendish was found.

### Cited Findings
- Moreno et al. 2021, *Int J Food Sci Technol* 56(3):1171-1183, doi:10.1111/ijfs.14851 (Colombia; 3 cultivars; about 7 fruits per time point, duplicate analyses; ripening at 24 °C, 60% RH). Table 2, mean ± SD, labelled % wet basis:
  - Pulp dry matter on day 0 / last day: Dominico Harton (plantain, AAB) 16.8 ± 0.2 / 13.1 ± 0.2 (day 10); Gros Michel (dessert, AAA) 10.7 ± 0.5 / 9.8 ± 0.1 (day 8); Guineo (cooking, AAA) 15.8 ± 0.2 / 13.8 ± 0.6 (day 5).
  - "Starch" on day 0 / last day: Dominico Harton 40.2 / 34.8; Gros Michel 23.5 / 20.4; Guineo 30.3 / 23.3.
  - Peel dry matter rose during ripening, e.g. Dominico Harton 10.2 to 32.2.
  - The text reports almost complete starch hydrolysis in Gros Michel, then Guineo, then Dominico Harton.
  - **Caution:** the table cannot be right as labelled. Starch (40%) is larger than dry matter (16.8%) on the same "wet basis". The Results text gives day-0 starch as 36.4 / 19.7 / 14.8%, which matches the table's TSS column. The pulp dry matter values are also far below typical plantain values. Use only the relative cultivar ranking.
  — [Moreno et al. 2021, PMC7984252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7984252)
- FHIA hybrids in Ghana (Sakyi-Dawson et al. 2008):
  - Starch and dry matter fell with ripening in all hybrids.
  - The cooking banana FHIA-03 went from about 750 to under 100 g/kg starch (evidently dry basis) and from 260 to 190 g/kg dry matter by stage 9.
  - Plantain hybrids had higher dry matter and starch than the cooking banana.
  - (search summary; full text not read; the summary flags internal inconsistency)
  — [UG Space record](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/fd0da138-728c-4dde-84f9-a655262f07a2)
- Plantains build up more starch (up to about 35% of pulp) and keep more undegraded starch when ripe, so they are less sweet than Cavendish. In ripe dessert banana, soluble sugars reach up to about 20% of pulp fresh weight, about 80% of it sucrose. (review in *Frontiers in Plant Science* 2019; search summary) — [Frontiers Plant Sci 2019, 10.3389/fpls.2019.00391](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2019.00391/pdf)
- Starch is 12-35% of dry weight at 4-8 weeks after flower emergence and falls to 0-15% in late maturation, depending on cultivar (search summary) — [INRAE HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-03675840v1/document)
- Cameroon dessert, cooking and plantain cultivars: firmness and starch fell, and reducing sugars and °Brix rose, from stage 1 to 5 (title/abstract level only) — [CIRAD record](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/dig-cirad-fr-391888)
- Indonesian cultivars: plantain starch is 20-30%, higher than in dessert bananas (search summary) — [Czech J Food Sci 2021](https://cjfs.agriculturejournals.cz/pdfs/cjf/2021/01/07.pdf)

### Inferences
- Carbs per gram depend mostly on cultivar group. Plantain pulp is typically about 30-35% carbohydrate fresh weight, against about 20-23% for dessert banana: roughly 1.5x. Ripeness mostly moves the starch/sugar split. The banana figures here are directional because the Colombian table is internally inconsistent.
- Pulp dry matter falls slightly as fruit ripens, partly because water moves from peel to pulp (peel DM rises). So per-gram carbohydrate falls a little in very ripe fruit even before respiratory losses.

### Gaps
- No primary data on Cavendish composition by country of origin or by cold-chain storage duration.
- No study gave n-based SDs for plantain versus Cavendish under one protocol on a reliable fresh-weight basis.

## CVs, ranges and variance partitioning: potato dry matter, wheat and rice protein, legume protein, fruit °Brix

### Takeaway
Which factor dominates depends on the crop and trait:
- **Rice protein:** management (N rate, transplanting date) dominates.
- **Wheat grain N:** genotype explains about half of the phenotypic variance after correcting for N, and G×site-year about a sixth.
- **Potato quality traits:** location, year and their interactions dominate, with cultivar at only 10-30%. Most variance sits within plots, between tubers.
- **Faba bean:** in a small trial with distinct cultivars, genotype dominated protein.

### Cited Findings
- **Wheat:** Mosleth et al. 2020, *Field Crops Res* 255:107896, doi:10.1016/j.fcr.2020.107896.
  - Design: 40 European genotypes, about 14 UK year-sites (2015/16-2017/18), N at 150 and 250 kg/ha. Protein = N x 5.7, by NIR.
  - Fractions of total phenotypic variance for grain N corrected for N: G 0.48, G×N 0.025, G×year-site 0.17, G×YS×N 0.01 (NS).
  - Fractions for grain protein deviation: G 0.30, G×N 0.03, G×YS 0.11; heritability 0.44.
  - No raw protein means or ranges were given.
  — [Mosleth et al. 2020, PMC7397848](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7397848)
- **Wheat:** Cooper et al. 2001 (Queensland, 272 lines, 3 sites x 2 years, with N/planting-time/irrigation regimes). The genotype × management variance was the largest G×E source for grain protein concentration and was larger than the genotypic variance (search summary) — [DAF Qld eprint 9950](https://era.daf.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/9950/)
- **Rice:** Lee et al. 2022, *Korean J Agric For Meteorol* 24(4):267-274, doi:10.5532/KJAFM.2022.24.4.267 (12 varieties, 2019-2021, 3 transplanting times x 3 N rates; brown rice).
  - Protein by N rate: 5.7% (0 N), 6.1% (9 kg N/10a), 6.8% (18 kg N/10a).
  - Protein by transplanting time: 5.7 (early), 5.9 (medium), 6.6% (late).
  - Contribution to protein: N 38.8%, transplanting time 13.7%, variety 8.2%, year 3.5%.
  — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65bbb72540e64bb074ab0ce1)
- **Rice:** a 20-genotype trial (6 locations x 2 years, 2021-2022) reportedly partitioned seed-protein variance as environment about 52.6%, genotype about 43.3% and G×E about 4.2%. This is from a search summary; the exact paper among the result links (ISGPB / Plant Archives / EJPB) was not confirmed. A 169-genotype panel found 5 genotypes above 10% protein across 3 locations. — [IJGPB](https://isgpb.org/journal/index.php/IJGPB/article/download/5069/3203); [EJPB](https://ejplantbreeding.org/index.php/EJPB/article/view/1002/468) (attribution unverified)
- **Faba bean:** Greveniotis et al. 2023, *Plants* 12(21):3769, doi:10.3390/plants12213769.
  - Design: 4 cultivars x 4 Greek sites x 2 years x 2 farming systems, 4 replicates.
  - Crude protein, % of dry matter: mean 27.80, min 23.93, max 31.64, SD 1.66 (CV about 6%, my calculation).
  - Mean squares: genotype 106.6, environment 24.4, G×E 0.08, error 0.05. Reported heritability 99.9%.
  — [PMC10648669](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10648669/)
- **Faba bean, typical range:** seed protein 24-35% of dry matter. In a 400-accession multi-location study, G×E strongly shaped protein (search summary) — [Frontiers Plant Sci 2024, 10.3389/fpls.2024.1480110](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2024.1480110/text)
- **Pea:** 306 RILs in 3 Italian environments; line means for protein 21.7-26.6%. Genetic effects were more than 2x larger than G×E, with no inverse genetic correlation between yield and protein (search summary) — [Frontiers Plant Sci 2021, 10.3389/fpls.2021.718713](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2021.718713/pdf)
- **Potato:** Veerman 2001 (Dutch cultivars, multiple locations and years).
  - Cultivar explained about 10-30% of variance in tuber quality traits (incl. dry matter); N and K explained 0-10%. Most variance came from location, year and their interactions.
  - Variance was larger within than between plots. Within lots, tubers from the same stem accounted for 80-90% of variance.
  - (search summary of AGRIS record; full text not read)
  — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/6511ad910777009829fa331d)
- **Potato:** Montenegro 2015-2017. Mean dry matter about 20.8% (Almera) versus 25.5% (Agria) over 3 years; genotype, year and location were significant (search summary) — [Plantarum repository](https://plantarum.izbis.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/675)
- **Potato:** Turkey (11 genotypes, 3 locations, 2 years). G×L×Y and G×L were significant for most traits; the authors advise multi-site, multi-year testing of dry matter (search summary) — [Ege repository](https://acikerisim.ege.edu.tr/items/8f6b9f8f-16eb-4882-868d-fd139da4b798)
- **Apple:**
  - 26 cultivars in Croatia: TSS 13.46-18.29 °Brix (search summary) — [Hrcak](https://hrcak.srce.hr/index.php/en/clanak/463917)
  - Within-cultivar fruit-to-fruit CV of SSC: about 13% for 'Aroma' and about 5% for 'Holsteiner Cox' (J Sci Food Agric 2013, search summary) — [Clemmensen papers](https://independent.academia.edu/LineClemmensen)

### Inferences
- **Protein in cereals:** environment and management usually matter as much as or more than cultivar. In rice, N fertiliser alone moved brown-rice protein from 5.7 to 6.8% (about ±9% around the mean). That shift exceeds the effect of switching variety.
- **Legume protein, ignoring G×E:** CVs are about 5-7% of dry matter for released cultivars. Diverse collections span about 24-35% (faba).
- **Potato dry matter:** the cultivar spread (about 20-26% DM) is comparable to the within-lot tuber-to-tuber spread. So a single potato sample is a poor estimate of the lot mean.

### Gaps
- No CV data was retrieved for maize, oats, grapes or citrus °Brix, or for nut composition.
- No primary potato paper with a numeric G/E/G×E split for dry matter was read in full.
- HEALTHGRAIN-specific protein data was not found.

## How much does water-content variation drive per-gram variability in fruits and vegetables?

### Takeaway
No primary study quantifying the "dilution" share of variance was found. The data above shows dry matter differences of several percentage points between cultivar groups (banana) and between potato cultivars (about 20-26% DM). These translate one-to-one into per-gram differences in all solids.

### Cited Findings
- Potato DM 20.8% (Almera) versus 25.5% (Agria) — [Plantarum repository](https://plantarum.izbis.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/675)
- Banana pulp DM changes and peel/pulp water movement during ripening — [PMC7984252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7984252)

### Inferences
- If dry matter goes from 20.8% to 25.5% in potato, every dry-matter component (starch, protein, fibre) and energy rises about 23% per 100 g fresh weight, even with identical dry-matter composition. For high-water vegetables (DM about 5-10%), a 1-point DM shift is a 10-20% relative change in all solids. This is arithmetic, not a sourced measurement.

### Gaps
- No variance decomposition (water versus dry-matter composition) was found for any fruit or vegetable.

## How does storage change macronutrients per gram (potato cold sweetening, moisture loss)?

### Takeaway
At 4 °C, potatoes accumulate reducing sugars (glucose, fructose) through cold-induced sweetening, and the amount depends strongly on cultivar. Sucrose may fall over long storage. Storing at 6.7-9 °C limits sweetening. No quantitative mg/g fresh-weight values were retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Galani Yamdeu et al. 2016, *J Food Biochem* 40(1):71-83, doi:10.1111/jfbc.12190: 11 Indian varieties stored at room temperature, 15 °C and 4 °C for 150 days. At 4 °C, reducing sugars, total soluble sugars, glucose and fructose rose and sucrose fell. K. Jyoti was CIS-tolerant and K. Badshah CIS-susceptible. The abstract gives no numbers. — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/161257/)
- Scottish study of 22 clones: after 4 °C storage, Record and Pentland Dell accumulated far more sugar than some breeding clones, which hardly accumulated any. Glucose mattered more than fructose for fry colour (search summary) — [Springer BF02358449](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02358449)
- European processing cultivars (Visse-Mansiaux et al.): Verdi, Lady Claire and Kiebitz kept glucose low for up to 4 months at 4 °C, while Pirol, Agria and Markies were CIS-susceptible (search summary; URL of primary not retrieved).
- Russet cultivars (2009): all sweetened quickly at 4.5 °C. Conditioning at 6.7-9 °C first reduced later sweetening (search summary) — [PHTnet PDF](https://www.phtnet.org/research/download/pdf/mb834.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cold sweetening converts starch to sugars, so it changes the starch/sugar split, which matters for glycaemic response and acrylamide. Total carbohydrate per gram changes little, except through respiration losses and water loss.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved:
  - numeric glucose/fructose/sucrose (mg/g FW) values by cultivar and storage time
  - percent weight (moisture) loss during potato storage
  - storage effects on apples/citrus (e.g. acid and sugar loss in controlled-atmosphere storage)
  - nut lipid changes in storage

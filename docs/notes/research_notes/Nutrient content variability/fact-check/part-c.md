# Fact-check part C: report lines 46–55

Section checked: "Storage, soil and sunlight push micronutrients two- to hundredfold" in
`docs/notes/reports/Nutrient content variability.md`.

Method: every source was opened at the primary level. For Lee & Kader (2000) and Mayer et al. (2021) the full text PDF was
read. For most other papers the publisher abstract was read through the Europe PMC REST API, looked up by DOI
(`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:"<doi>"&resultType=core&format=json`). Lu et al. (2007)
was read on its PMC page. Lee & Kader full text came from the authors' UC Davis copy, archived at
`https://web.archive.org/web/20181222224638/http://ucce.ucdavis.edu:80/files/datastore/234-17.pdf`. That is the paper as
published in Postharvest Biol. Technol. 20:207–220.

## Uncited framing checked against the evidence above

- **Line 46, heading "two- to hundredfold".** The largest ratio documented by the cited sources is about 67-fold for iodine in
  fish (18 to 1,210 µg/100 g). Brazil-nut selenium is about 33-fold. "Trace to 9 mg" in sweet potato has no finite ratio.
  Marles reports copper "natural range of variation" figures of 1,400–20,600%, but these are spreads across foods, not within
  one food. "Hundredfold" is therefore a slight round-up. "Two- to nearly hundredfold" or "two- to fiftyfold-plus" would be
  safer. This is advisory only; the heading cites no source.
- **Line 50, "two- to fourfold across cultivars".** This is consistent with the cited ranges: strawberry 3.7×, pepper 2.6×,
  carrot β-carotene 2.2×, spinach folate 3.2×.

# Sampling CVs fitted to USDA SR28

- **Date:** 2026-10-22
- **Status:** accepted
- **Instructions:** [`docs/instructions/design.md`](../instructions/design.md); model from [`docs/notes/error-handling.md`](../notes/error-handling.md)

## Context

[error-model](2026-10-22-error-model.md) shipped judgement priors for the CVs. The user asked to fit them to USDA data.

## Decision

`data/scripts/fit_cvs.py` (under `docs/notes/research_notes/Nutrient content variability/`) writes
`data/sr28_fitted_cvs.csv`, and `app/src/errors.js` takes its values (2 significant digits).

- Per food and nutrient: CV = SE·√n / mean, from records with n ≥ 3 and SE > 0 (SE = 0 is a placeholder).
- A food's CV for a kind is the median over that kind's nutrients. A class's CV is the median over its foods; a class
  with fewer than 5 foods for a kind falls back to the all-foods median.
- Energy and carbohydrate have almost no SE in SR28, so they come from the analysed proximates, treated as
  independent. Carbohydrate is by difference (C = 100 − W − P − F − A), so Var C = Var W + Var P + Var F + Var A and
  Var E = 16 Var W + 16 Var A + 25 Var F. For foods with ≤ 1 g carbohydrate, Var E = 16 Var P + 81 Var F.
- SR28 food groups map to classes (dairy 0100 split into eggs by name; baked products 1800 split into sweets by name;
  snacks, ethnic foods and Native American foods unused).
- Unknown = the 75th percentile over all foods.

Effect on the user's 2026-10-21: 1947 kcal, −213/+229 → −126/+131 at 90%.

## Alternatives considered

- **Unknown = the widest class's median** — dominated by nutrients a food barely has (carbs in meat 1.27, protein in
  oils 0.37).
- **Scale by √3 for blended samples** — SR28 often analyses blends of several items, so its CVs understate single
  items. Not applied: multi-study records carry between-study scatter in the other direction, and the size of either
  is unknown per food.
- **FoodData Central Foundation Foods** (per-sample results) — not downloaded; SR28 already covers ~3400 foods.

## Consequences

- Energy CVs are now a few percent in most classes (meat 8%, fish 10%, unknown 8%): a well-identified food. Cut,
  trim, ripeness and brand are not in them.
- Kinds with few foods: vitamin D (28), caffeine (16), alcohol (33).
- Classes with tiny amounts of a nutrient get large CVs for it (meat carbohydrate 1.3); they act on near-zero values.

## Open questions

- Should blended-sample CVs be scaled up (√3 ≈ 1.7×)?
